EXPERIMENT C.  RECALIBRATION. DOES A SINGLE NUMBER FIX IT, AND AT WHAT COST
============================================================================================================
July pipeline step 3: recalibrate and show the ratio move toward 1.
Two corrections, and the difference between them is what you can actually ship.
  (a) PRIOR SHIFT: add logit(target prev) - logit(source prev). Needs only the
      target PREVALENCE, which a deploying hospital knows. Deployable.
  (b) INTERCEPT REFIT: fit the intercept on the target labels. Needs outcomes
      from the target site. This is the ceiling, not a shippable method.

============================================================================================================
EXPERIMENT D.  WHAT HAPPENS AT SCREENING PREVALENCE
============================================================================================================
This cohort is INPATIENT ENDOCRINOLOGY. Inclusion was inpatients in the
Department of Endocrinology who had both fundus imaging and carotid ultrasound.
That is why 70.8 percent of them have thickened CIMT. A retinal camera used for
SCREENING meets a far healthier population, so the shift at deployment is much
larger than the 0.731 to 0.500 measured in experiment A.

Method: resample the 300 held out patients to a target prevalence, with
replacement, 2000 draws. This MEASURES the shift rather than assuming it.
Held out positives and negatives available: 150 and 150.

The uncorrected model is a risk INFLATOR, and the healthier the population the
worse it gets. Nothing in an AUC would tell you that.

THE ASSUMPTION IN THIS TABLE, AND IT IS A REAL LIMIT. Resampling changes the MIX
of cases and controls. It cannot change what a case or a control LOOKS like. A
real screening population has milder disease in its positives and healthier
negatives than an endocrinology ward does, and this cannot capture that. So read
these as the PREVALENCE COMPONENT of the deployment shift, isolated, not as a
forecast of a screening study. The direction of the residual is not known and is
not claimed. What is solid: the component that IS measured here is large, and one
number removes most of it.

============================================================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW
============================================================================================================
DOES: whether discrimination and calibration come apart under the case mix
      shift that is already built into this published benchmark, and whether
      a one parameter correction closes the calibration gap.
DOES NOT: anything about ethnicity or skin tone. There is no such column here.
      The fundus luminance rows are a method check on an image property that is
      confounded with camera exposure. They are not a fairness result.
DOES NOT: predict cardiovascular events. The label is carotid wall thickness at
      a 0.9 mm cut, which is a marker, not an outcome. 13.2 percent of patients
      sit exactly on that cut.

THE ONE LINE THAT CONNECTS THIS TO THE ETHNICITY QUESTION, and it is the dataset
authors saying it, not us. They justify the 0.9 mm cut from a Chinese cohort and
explicitly acknowledge THE VARIABILITY OF CIMT THRESHOLDS ACROSS ETHNICITIES AND
AGE GROUPS. So on this task the ETHNICITY DEPENDENCE IS IN THE LABEL DEFINITION,
before any model exists. Transport this model to a population with a different
appropriate threshold and you have label shift on top of the prevalence shift
measured above. That is the thing worth studying, and it needs a cohort with a
recorded ethnicity column, which this one does not have.
